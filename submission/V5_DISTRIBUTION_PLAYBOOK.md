**The competition.** 1,218 of 6,292 registrants had submitted at 12:00 IST today. Reach is the one judged component you can still move.

**Positioning: one message, a different lead per audience.**
- **Instagram and YouTube (the public feeds):** "The ₹10 protein fact → a real tool for students on a budget, built with AI." The video already opens on this fact. It's the most *sendable* idea we have: a surprising number that gym-going students argue about and forward to friends. Sends are the signal Instagram weights most for reaching non-followers.

## 3. Platform research: what is known
### Instagram
- **Ranking signals:**
  - Mosseri (Jan 2025): "the top three signals… are watch time, likes and sends… pay close attention to average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." Sends are "slightly more important" for unconnected (non-follower) content [OFFICIAL].
  - Watch time counts both seconds and % watched [OFFICIAL, Feb 2025].
  - The "sends = 3–5× likes" figure is **unsupported** [REPORTED debunks].
- **Hashtags:**
  - Capped at **5 per post/Reel** since Dec 2025 [REPORTED, @Creators announcement].
  - Mosseri: hashtags help say what a post is about and help search, but don't increase reach; "a few specific tags actually perform better than a long list of generic ones" [OFFICIAL].
  - → No #fyp / #viral / #explorepage: there's no evidence they help, and they burn 1 of your 5 slots.
- **Captions and search:**
  - Since 10 July 2025, public posts from **professional accounts (18+)** can be indexed by Google and Bing, including caption text [REPORTED, consistent across sources].
  - Keywords belong in the caption, ideally the first line [CREATOR].
  - Small experiments show keyword captions beating hashtag-only posts, but these are weak evidence [CREATOR].
- **Originality:** Reels made in other apps aren't demoted unless watermarked (Mosseri, Oct 2026) [OFFICIAL]. Aggregators are excluded from recommendations (2026) [REPORTED]. → Our file is original and watermark-free ✅.
- **Skip rate** (the share of viewers who skip in the first 3 s) is now an Insights metric. Its use as a *ranking* signal is unconfirmed [OFFICIAL metric / REPORTED caveat].
- **Trial Reels:**
  - They need **≥200 followers on a professional account** [REPORTED via Help Center], and they don't appear on your profile until shared.
  - → Not usable here: @siddmakesstuff has 6 followers, and the competition needs a public post link.
- **Links:**
  - Reel captions aren't clickable for standard accounts.
  - The Story **link sticker** works for everyone.
  - The bio link is clickable [REPORTED].
  - The rules still require the app link *in the post*, so put it in the caption as plain text.
- **Collab posts:**
  - @embarko.ai's grid already contains Collab reels with creators (codernormie: 1,033 likes and 444 comments; techdevendra47; learnwithvalerio), checked today.
  - → Inviting @embarko.ai as a collaborator is a proven, legitimate path onto their grid. They may decline; nothing is lost.

### YouTube Shorts
- **Feed distribution:** YouTube measures "How many chose to view" against swiped away, plus average view duration and % viewed [OFFICIAL Help metric]. The Shorts lead said hashtags are "not required" [REPORTED].
- **Metadata:**
  - "Title, thumbnail, and description are more important… tags play a minimal role… useful if commonly misspelled" [OFFICIAL Help 146402].
  - Titles matter mainly for search and audience matching. Literal keyword titles work for search [CREATOR].
- **Links:** URLs in Shorts descriptions and comments are **not clickable** (since Aug 2023). Channel-profile links are clickable [OFFICIAL Help 13748639]. → Put the app link on the channel page too.
- **Thumbnails:**
  - Custom Shorts thumbnails rolled out from Jul 2026 (desktop, partners first).
  - Mobile upload was reported from late Nov 2026; availability varies.
  - The frame picker works for everyone [REPORTED].
- **Views:** every start or replay counts as a view [OFFICIAL change of 31 Mar 2025]. Your loop-friendly 46 s cut benefits.

### Timing
- No platform publishes a universal "best time." For a 6-follower account, followers' active hours barely matter. What matters is the **first hour of real engagement**, from people you can personally reach [INFERENCE from the signals above].
- **Decision:** post **today, Saturday 28 Nov, at 18:30 IST**, when Indian students are on their phones and you can stay online for 90 minutes. Waiting until tomorrow wastes judging-window hours; posting right now (midday Saturday) wastes your first-hour push.

## The number that started it
At typical Indian shop prices, ₹10 buys about 37 g of protein as soya chunks and about 4 g as whey. That's roughly 9× more per rupee. But nobody is going to eat half a kilo of soya every day, so the real problem isn't "what's cheapest", it's "what's the cheapest *realistic* plate".

## What the app does
You answer five questions: weight, goal, diet (veg, veg + eggs, non-veg), what you can cook with (nothing, a kettle, a full kitchen) and how many mess meals you eat. The app works out your daily protein target, subtracts what your mess meals already give you, and plans the top-up. In my demo, a 68 kg student who eats eggs and has two mess meals needs a 79 g top-up, and the cheapest plan that fully covers it costs ₹53 a day.

Then you can drag the daily budget and watch the plate rebuild, skip foods you hate, pin foods you always want, change prices to match your shop, and get a weekly shopping list.

## How it works
Under the hood it's an exact optimiser, not a heuristic: a bounded-knapsack dynamic programme over real foods and real pack sizes, limited to at most five foods so the plan stays practical. One table answers both "the cheapest plan for 79 g" and "the most protein for ₹49", which is why the budget slider can re-plan instantly. Everything runs in the browser, with nothing to sign up for and nothing stored on a server.

## Building it with Claude Code
I built the app with Claude Code: React, TypeScript and Vite, with unit tests for the optimiser and end-to-end tests on a phone-sized screen. The most useful part wasn't the code generation. It was being able to iterate quickly on the hard questions: what "cheapest" means when you can only buy whole packs, and how to present a plan that a student will actually follow.
